# Storyboard Table Guofeng Cyber 3D Constraints - Guofeng Cyber 3D - Technique Reference
---

## 1. Purpose of the Storyboard Table
The storyboard table is the director's core tool for translating a script into shot language. The following provides only the constraint specifications for the guofeng cyber 3D style at the storyboard-table level. **The core principle is "guofeng core first, cyber form in support" — whether in a classical ancient-style scene or a modern urban scene, both must hold to Eastern classical elegance and narrative logic, with cyber elements strictly forbidden from overshadowing the core.**

---

## 2. Core Principles Shared by Both Scene Types (Mandatory for Both Ancient-Style/Urban Scenes)
1.  **Style-ratio iron rule**: In every shot, core guofeng elements (traditional color, Eastern form, classical poise, Chinese aesthetics) must occupy >=70% of the frame, with cyber-tech elements occupying <=30% — pure Western cyber and coreless tech piling-on are forbidden
2.  **Dual-scene adaptation logic**:
    - Classical ancient-style scenes: use traditional Chinese architecture/costume/ritual as the base, with cyber elements as narrative accents that do not disrupt the completeness of the ancient-style worldview
    - Modern urban scenes: use new-Chinese aesthetics and the Eastern traditional core as the base, fusing cyber elements with the urban scene; grounded guofeng symbols (new-Chinese form, traditional color, Eastern ritual, guofeng motifs) must be embedded — pure urban cyber without a guofeng core is forbidden
3.  **Unified pacing rule**: Regardless of ancient-style/urban scene, the entire film defaults to maintaining Eastern elegant, unhurried pacing; only passages with strong narrative conflict may temporarily break this, and must be marked with a clear duration and trigger logic

---

## 3. Lighting and Mood (Full Adaptation for Both Ancient-Style/Urban Scenes)
- **Unified lighting within the same scene** — A single scene should not feature more than two lighting schemes, unless there is a clear narrative turning point (such as a day-to-night transition, a natural lighting change like clear-to-rainy, or a switch between reality and a digital illusion); cyber-exclusive lighting (neon, holographic projection, self-glowing electronic screens, glowing cybernetic-body components) must be folded into the same scene's unified lighting scheme. In **ancient-style scenes**, the neon primary color and electronic-light color temperature within the same scene must stay consistent with the ancient-style light source (lanterns, candlelight, natural skylight); in **urban scenes**, the cyber light source within the same scene must have a unified color temperature with the base Chinese light source (new-Chinese pendant lamp, guofeng street lamp, building Chinese-style outline light) — mismatched colored light intruding with no narrative logic is forbidden
- **Mood-palette binding** — Each scene must be bound to at least 1 mood scenario, with full coverage for both scene types:
  - Ancient-style base set: courtly luxury / landscape mood / boudoir tenderness / wuxia severity / festive celebration / moonlit serenity
  - Guofeng-cyber-exclusive set (shared by ancient-style/urban): cyber jianghu severity / digital-illusion dreaminess / cyber court power struggle / wasteland ancient-building desolation / neon night-market prosperity / guofeng urban workplace / urban ancient-alley mystery
  and maintain primary-plus-secondary color continuity within the shot group; the color-palette rule is mandatory as "guofeng traditional color as the base," with core adaptation color reference: vermilion paired with neon cyan, stone-blue paired with fluorescent pink, ink black paired with electronic purple, gilded paired with ice blue — avoiding any loss of the guofeng core
- **3D rendering lighting specification** — When describing lighting, clearly state the volumetric-light direction and intensity, the ambient-occlusion layering, and the depth-of-field blur range. The reflection/refraction effect of PBR materials under different lighting is key to the image's texture quality; dual-scene-exclusive rendering specifications:
  - Ancient-style scenes: must specify the PBR material parameters of the ancient building's wood structure/brick-and-stone/silk/jade, and simultaneously note the semi-transparent volumetric-light tier of holographic projections, the color-spill control of neon tubing, and the reflection effect of metal components
  - Urban scenes: must specify the PBR material parameters of the new-Chinese building's glass curtain wall/concrete/metal panel, and simultaneously note the light-and-shadow reflection of guofeng motifs etched into glass, the volumetric-light scattering of guofeng holographic advertisements, the intensity and range of the building's Chinese-style outline light, and the glow radius of cybernetic-body/wearable-device glowing elements
  All lighting descriptions must clearly specify the fusion logic between guofeng material lighting and cyber material lighting, avoiding disconnect between the two
- **Warm-cool tone matching narrative stage** — Severe/melancholic/oppressive/mystery passages use cool-toned light (indigo + ink black + low-saturation neon cyan), with high-saturation warm tones like vermilion/golden yellow forbidden; warm tones (vermilion + golden yellow + rouge + low-saturation neon pink/orange) are used only in luxurious, tender, and celebratory passages; electronic glitch light and flicker may only be used at strong narrative conflict points (such as a system crash or memory flashback), and must be marked with a duration and frequency — **overuse is forbidden in both ancient-style/urban scenes**
- **Lighting transitions are an advanced technique** — Shared transition specification for both scenes, must mark the lighting-change point:
  - Ancient-style scenes: gauze-window diffuse light gradually transitioning to moonlit serenity = the passage of time from day to night; lantern warm light gradually transitioning to holographic-projection cool light = the switch from reality to a digital illusion
  - Urban scenes: Chinese-style street-lamp warm light gradually transitioning to office-tower neon cool light = the scene switch from daily life to workplace; ancient-alley lattice-window light and shadow gradually transitioning to skyscraper glass-curtain-wall light and shadow = the narrative passage from tradition to modernity
  All lighting transitions must maintain an unhurried pace, matching the overall guofeng elegant temperament

## 4. Environmental Dynamics (Full Adaptation for Both Ancient-Style/Urban Scenes)
- **Environmental dynamics add a sense of breathing life to the frame** — Arrange at least one shot with environmental dynamics every 3-4 shots to avoid the image feeling "dead"; **mandatory dual-scene requirement**: every 3-4 shots must simultaneously balance the expression of "core guofeng dynamic elements" + "cyber-tech dynamic elements" in fusion — a single scene element in isolation is forbidden:
  - Ancient-style scene adaptation: falling petals synchronized with drifting holographic particles, flickering candlelight synchronized with faint electronic-screen glow, swaying gauze curtains synchronized with flowing holographic-projection light and shadow, wind through the bamboo forest synchronized with a breathing-light effect from neon tubing among the bamboo
  - Urban scene adaptation: a new-Chinese canvas awning fluttering in the wind synchronized with flowing neon light under the eaves, holographic guofeng-calligraphy particles drifting in the commercial district synchronized with falling leaves in the sky, the light-and-shadow change of guofeng motifs on an office-tower glass curtain wall synchronized with the slight movement of indoor Chinese potted-plant leaves, the breathing-light effect of guofeng light boxes in a subway passage synchronized with the wind effect of a passing train
- **Guofeng environmental elements take priority** — Environmental dynamics must primarily use elements from within the guofeng worldview, with full coverage for both scenes:
  - Ancient-style scene compliant elements: falling flowers, drifting catkins, wisps of smoke, flowing water, wind through the bamboo forest, flickering candlelight, swaying gauze curtains, rippling water
  - Urban scene compliant elements: new-Chinese structural elements moving in the wind, holographic guofeng-motif dynamics, swaying Chinese potted-plant branches and leaves, swaying light and shadow of guofeng lanterns/street lamps, flowing Chinese water features, fluttering rice-paper/silk materials
  Cyber dynamic elements are limited to content compliant within the guofeng cyber worldview: the dynamic motion of guofeng motifs in holographic projections, the breathing-light effect of neon tubing, the flowing calligraphy/landscape forms of digital particles, the slow movement of mechanical components, the faint flicker of cybernetic-body elements, the slight hovering sway of floating guofeng buildings; **shared prohibition for both scenes**: street graffiti, purely Western advertisement billboards, modern cars without a guofeng core, Western cyber machinery, and other jarring elements are forbidden
- **3D particle and volumetric effects** — Fully leverage the advantages of 3D rendering to achieve a fusion of volumetric effects between guofeng and cyber elements, with dual-scene adaptation specifications:
  - Ancient-style scenes: volumetric-light scattering of mist, PBR reflection of ancient buildings on the water surface, the physical falling trajectory of petals, volumetric rendering of smoke, simultaneously nested with holographic calligraphy particles and holographic petal-trail light effects
  - Urban scenes: volumetric-light scattering of rain and mist, PBR reflection of guofeng motifs on the glass curtain wall, the physical falling trajectory of leaves, volumetric rendering of holographic projections, simultaneously nested with holographic guofeng-landscape particles and the guofeng-outline scattering of neon light effects
  All particle effects must specify their quantity, volumetric density, and movement speed, suited to the guofeng elegant slow pace — **fast, chaotic, random particle effects are forbidden in both scene types**

## 5. Ancient-Style Action Pacing (Full Adaptation for Both Ancient-Style/Urban Scenes)
- **Core pacing of ancient-style action: restrained and steady** — All character actions default to slow speed; rising, turning, and raising a hand should all be marked "slow"; cyber-exclusive actions (cybernetic-body extension, holographic operation, mechanical-component extension/retraction) also default to a slow, graceful speed — **only in passages of strong narrative conflict, such as combat or crisis, may fast action be marked, and the fast-action duration must not exceed 30% of a single shot's total length**; **supplementary specification for urban scenes**: even in daily commute, workplace, or social scenes, character action must still maintain an Eastern steady temperament — abrupt small steps and large fast movements are forbidden, maintaining the guofeng reserved temperament throughout, with exaggerated mechanical-showoff moves forbidden
- **Costume dynamics** — The flutter of guofeng costume is a natural dynamic asset; the drape and flutter effect of cloth simulation in 3D rendering should be reflected in the frame description, adding a "living" feel to the image; dual-scene adaptation specification:
  - Ancient-style scenes: must specify the silk/cotton-linen/gauze drape and flutter effect of traditional ancient-style costume (wide sleeves, skirt hem, pibo shawl), while simultaneously noting the linked dynamics of neon tubing, glowing motifs, and mechanical accessories on the costume
  - Urban scenes: must specify the drape and flutter effect of new-Chinese costume (modified qipao, new-Chinese suit, guofeng daily wear), while simultaneously noting the linked dynamics of glowing frog buttons, mechanical cuffs, holographic motifs, and guofeng accessories on the costume
  Shared prohibition for both scenes: cyber costume components must not disrupt the guofeng costume's core form and Eastern beauty
- **Bearing constraint** — Shared bearing specification for both scenes: characters' conduct must conform to Eastern classical temperament — walking steady and unhurried, gestures reserved and restrained, with no modern body language (such as shrugging, throwing up hands, leg-shaking, large hand-waving, or exaggerated body swaying); **supplementary specification for urban scenes**: cyber-related body movements (holographic-panel operation, access-card swiping, cybernetic-body adjustment, electronic-device operation) must remain reserved and graceful, with steady, precise operating motions — exaggerated large gestures and Western body language are forbidden; even mechanical-cybernetic-body characters must have body movements that fit the guofeng classical poise, avoiding a cold, stiff mechanical feel
- **3D animation texture** — Action transitions should be natural and fluid, expressing the refined feel of 3D animation. Avoid stiff pose switches, paying attention to the natural arc of joints and weight shift; supplementary specification for both scenes: the transition between traditional body movement and cyber mechanical movement must be naturally smooth with no stutter or frame skipping; cybernetic-body movement must match the body's center of gravity and joint-movement logic; holographic-operation movement must have clear spatial positioning and feedback animation, balancing 3D animation's realistic texture with the guofeng Eastern freehand-brushwork mood

## 6. Camera Movement and Transition Prohibitions (Full Adaptation for Both Ancient-Style/Urban Scenes)
- **Fast camera movement forbidden** — Whip pans, sudden push-ins, handheld shake, high-speed pass-throughs, strobe jump cuts, and first-person fast running shots all conflict with the elegant temperament of guofeng 3D — **forbidden by default throughout both ancient-style/urban scenes**; only in a single shot with strong narrative conflict (explosion, system crash, life-and-death combat) may an extremely short fast camera move be used, and its duration must not exceed 20% of that shot's total length — showing off technique at the expense of Eastern elegance is forbidden
- **Fancy transitions forbidden** — Swipe transitions, spins, venetian blinds, flash-white hard cuts, illogical glitch-art transitions, and zoom-bounce transitions are all incompatible with this style — **forbidden throughout both ancient-style/urban scenes**; only transitions matching the guofeng cyber temperament may be used: lighting gradient transitions, matched-element transitions (an ancient building's flying eave matched to a cyber flying eave, a jade pendant matched to a guofeng access card, a lantern matched to a guofeng street lamp), spatial-depth push/pull transitions, and slow dissolve transitions — the transition pace must be unhurried, matching the overall guofeng temperament
- **Skillful use of 3D spatial camera movement (dual-scene adaptation specification)** — May use the spatial depth of a 3D scene for slow push/pull or orbiting camera movement, but must maintain a steady, graceful pace — showy pass-through/flying camera movements are forbidden; dual-scene-exclusive camera-movement guidance:
  - Ancient-style scenes: may orbit slowly around an ancient courtyard, presenting the fusion of ancient architecture and cyber elements; slowly push in from the courtyard gate, gradually revealing the cyberized ancient interior in the depth — default camera speed <=0.5 m/s
  - Urban scenes: may push in slowly from an urban ancient-alley's Chinese-style gate, gradually revealing skyscrapers and a neon commercial district embedded with guofeng elements in the depth; may orbit slowly around a new-Chinese office tower, simultaneously presenting the building's Chinese flying eaves and the cyber light and shadow of the glass curtain wall; camera movement throughout must serve narrative and emotional expression rather than pure effects display, with a default push/pull or orbit speed of <=0.5 m/s
